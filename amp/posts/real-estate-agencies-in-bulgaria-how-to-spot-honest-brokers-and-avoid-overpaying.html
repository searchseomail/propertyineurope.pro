Always check if the agency is a member of the National Real Estate Association. If they refuse to provide a clear breakdown of their commission in writing before you sign a preliminary contract, walk away immediately. It is also crucial to understand the legal status of the building, so check out this guide on what is Act 16 in Bulgaria before putting down any deposit.

Never pay a commission until the final notary deed is signed. Some brokers try to pressure you for a 'reservation fee' that is non-refundable; avoid those like the plague. If you are new to the market, read this buying property in Bulgaria as a foreigner guide to understand the standard steps and avoid common pitfalls.

A major red flag is a broker who pushes you to use 'their' lawyer. Always hire an independent attorney who has no ties to the agency to conduct a thorough property title check. This is non-negotiable if you want to avoid hidden encumbrances.

When negotiating, remember that the listed price on sites like Imot.bg is usually inflated. You can often shave off 5-10% if you are a cash buyer and show you are ready to move quickly. Don't be afraid to lowball if the property has been on the market for months.

I learned the hard way that some agencies try to charge both the buyer and the seller a commission. Ask them point-blank if they are representing the seller's interests, which they almost always are. Clarify your role as a client versus a customer early on.
