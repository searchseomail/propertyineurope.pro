Legal Process of Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria: Lawyer Duties vs. Notary Public

ember_quest
Topic Starter
Posted: 30.01.2025 07:52
I am currently in the process of purchasing an apartment in Sofia and am confused about the distinct roles of the legal professionals involved. While I know a notary public is mandatory for the title transfer, I am unsure if hiring a separate lawyer is strictly necessary to protect my interests. Could someone clarify the specific scope of a lawyer's due diligence versus what a notary actually verifies during the signing process? I want to make sure I am not missing any critical checks before finalizing the deal.

fable_runner
Replied: 05.03.2025 15:27
The notary is there to formalize the transaction and check basic documentation, but they don't work for you. You absolutely need a lawyer to perform deep due diligence on the property history, potential liens, and hidden debts. Check out this guide for foreigners to see why the legal check is crucial.
misty_anchor
Replied: 15.03.2025 07:26
Think of the notary as a neutral party who ensures the paperwork is signed correctly according to law. Your lawyer is your advocate. They will investigate if the seller has the legal right to sell and if there are any active legal disputes attached to the apartment.
ember_quest
Replied: 16.03.2025 14:19
I bought in Sofia last year and hired a lawyer. The notary didn't catch a discrepancy in the cadastral map that my lawyer found immediately. It saved me a lot of stress later on.
azure_drift
Replied: 15.04.2025 07:35
Don't skip the lawyer. The notary checks if the seller owns the property on paper, but they won't dig into the history of the building or verify if the seller is currently being sued by creditors, which could jeopardize your purchase.
velvet_shift
Replied: 09.08.2025 08:06
Many people confuse the two, but they serve completely different purposes. If you are looking for a cheaper option, be careful; sometimes low prices mask legal issues. Read more about identifying these risks here: Cheap Apartments: Bargain or Money Pit?
lunar_byte
Replied: 04.10.2025 16:08
The notary only verifies the identity of the parties and the validity of the Title Deed transfer. They do not represent your financial or legal interests if something goes wrong after you pay.
frost_byte
Replied: 16.10.2025 08:22
A lawyer will draft the Preliminary Contract, which is arguably the most important stage. This is where you set the penalties if the seller backs out. The notary only sees the final Title Deed.
iron_petal
Replied: 19.10.2025 11:36
Always hire an independent lawyer. Never use the one recommended by the real estate agent or the seller, as there could be a conflict of interest. You want someone representing only your side.
mercury_glow
Replied: 05.11.2025 09:01
The notary is mandatory by law for the conveyance of real estate, but they are not liable if the property has hidden legal flaws. Your lawyer is your insurance policy against those risks.
silent_voyager
Replied: 11.01.2026 06:23
Make sure your lawyer also checks the status of the building's management. You don't want to buy a place only to find out there are massive unresolved issues with the homeowners' association or maintenance debts.
orbit_bound
Replied: 17.01.2026 14:33
It is common for foreigners to feel overwhelmed by the process. A lawyer handles the 'Property Certificate' check, which shows all encumbrances. The notary doesn't do this with the same level of scrutiny as your own counsel.
