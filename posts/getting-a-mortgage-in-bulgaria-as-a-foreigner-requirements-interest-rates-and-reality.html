Getting a Mortgage in Bulgaria as a Foreigner: Requirements, Interest Rates, and Reality

static_dreamer
Topic Starter
Posted: 10.01.2025 09:40
I am looking into purchasing a property in Bulgaria and was wondering if anyone has successfully secured a mortgage as a non-resident recently. What are the typical down payment requirements and current interest rates for foreigners compared to locals? I would also appreciate any insights on the documentation process and whether banks are generally hesitant to lend to expats. Any advice on recommended banks or pitfalls to avoid would be greatly appreciated.

Replies (13)

azure_drift
Replied: 13.01.2025 07:26
I managed to get a mortgage last year. As a non-resident, the biggest hurdle is definitely the initial paperwork. Most banks require at least a 30% down payment compared to the 15-20% locals might get. Interest rates are currently hovering between 2.5% and 4%, but it depends heavily on your income source.
frost_byte
Replied: 16.02.2025 12:32
Don't expect it to be easy. Many banks are quite hesitant with expats unless you have a permanent residence permit. If you are struggling with the basics, check this guide on opening a bank account in Bulgaria first, as you'll need that before even discussing a loan.
solar_tide
Replied: 15.03.2025 08:39
I spoke to three major banks in Sofia. They all asked for proof of local income. If your income is in a foreign currency, they usually apply a significant discount to your calculated purchasing power, which makes the loan amount much lower than you'd expect.
zenith_flow
Replied: 24.03.2025 01:14
It is possible, but be prepared for a long process. The documentation is intense—you'll need translations of everything, and you must have a local lawyer to handle the property checks. Understanding the legal process of buying real estate in Bulgaria is essential before you put down any deposit.
velvet_coder
Replied: 14.04.2025 01:22
Banks here are very conservative. They care more about your employment contract in Bulgaria than your savings abroad. If you're a freelancer or remote worker, you might find it almost impossible to get approved.
echo_point
Replied: 25.05.2025 13:14
My advice: use a mortgage broker. They have relationships with specific branch managers who are more open to working with foreigners. It saved me a lot of time and frustration.
ember_quest
Replied: 16.06.2025 01:41
Interest rates are rising, so don't bank on the low rates you might have seen online a year ago. Also, keep in mind that the property valuation by the bank is usually lower than the market price, meaning you end up needing more cash on hand.
saffron_sky
Replied: 27.07.2025 11:07
I bought a place in Bansko last year. Honestly, I found it easier to just pay cash. If you do go for a mortgage, be prepared for the bank to demand an appraisal from their own list of certified valuers.
pixel_wanderer
Replied: 13.09.2025 10:15
One thing people forget is the ongoing cost of the property. Before you commit to a mortgage, make sure you look into heating costs in Bulgaria during winter, as these can be a massive shock if the building has poor insulation.
opal_circuit
Replied: 26.10.2025 00:23
I'm an EU citizen and it was still a headache. They treated me almost the same as a non-EU foreigner. Expect to provide at least 6 months of bank statements and a clean credit report from your home country.
atlas_notes
Replied: 02.11.2025 05:39
Avoid the small regional banks if you are a foreigner; they often don't have the internal protocols to handle non-resident applications. Stick to the big players like DSK, UniCredit, or UBB.
misty_anchor
Replied: 09.11.2025 11:31
Is it worth it? The bureaucracy is insane. I spent three months just getting the bank to accept my foreign income proof. If you don't have a residence card, forget about a standard mortgage; you might be looking at private lending which has much higher interest rates.
zenith_flow
Replied: 09.01.2026 14:12
Just make sure your lawyer is independent. Do not use the lawyer recommended by the seller or the real estate agency. You need someone strictly representing your interests when dealing with the bank's requirements.
