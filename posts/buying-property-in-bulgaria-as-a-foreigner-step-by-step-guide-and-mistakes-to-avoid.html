Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner: Step-by-Step Guide and Mistakes to Avoid

midnight_pulse
Topic Starter
Posted: 17.01.2025 04:32
I am seriously considering investing in a holiday apartment in Bansko or along the Black Sea coast, but I am overwhelmed by the legal complexities involved for non-EU citizens. Could someone provide a clear, step-by-step breakdown of the purchase process, including necessary documentation and tax obligations? I am particularly concerned about potential pitfalls or common scams that foreigners often fall victim to during the transaction. Any advice on finding a reliable lawyer and navigating the notary process would be greatly appreciated.

As a non-EU citizen, the most important thing to know is that you can buy an apartment in your own name as an individual, but you cannot own land. If you buy a house with a plot, you must register a Bulgarian company to hold the title. Always hire an independent lawyer—never use the one recommended by the estate agent.
dusk_pilot
Replied: 23.08.2025 08:33
I bought in Bansko last year. The process is straightforward: you sign a Preliminary Contract, pay a deposit, and then finalize at the notary. The biggest pitfall is the 'Act 16' certification. Never buy a property that doesn't have it, or you might end up paying residential electricity rates or face issues with basic utilities.
glass_horizon
Replied: 21.09.2025 10:39
Check the property's 'Certificate of Encumbrances' (Tezhesti) before paying a single lev. This document will show if there are any outstanding mortgages or legal disputes linked to the apartment. You can get this from the Property Register, and a good lawyer will handle this for you.
nova_sketch
Replied: 24.10.2025 11:50
Don't fall for the 'too good to be true' prices on the Black Sea coast. Often, these are older buildings with massive maintenance fees or structural issues. Always ask for the maintenance fee breakdown in writing before you commit to anything.
canyon_haze
Replied: 20.11.2025 11:46
Finding a reliable lawyer is key. Look for firms that specialize in conveyancing for foreigners and are based in Sofia or the major coastal cities, rather than relying on local contacts in the resort town. Ensure they are registered with the Bulgarian Bar Association.
kinetic_soul
Replied: 07.12.2025 14:34
Budget for the 'hidden' costs: notary fees, local taxes, and agency commission usually add about 5-8% on top of the purchase price. Also, remember that you must pay annual property taxes to the local municipality, which is usually a small amount but must be paid on time to avoid penalties.
ember_flow
Replied: 23.12.2025 04:42
Beware of scams involving 'pre-construction' deals. Many developers promise completion dates that they never meet. If you decide to invest in a new build, make sure the contract has heavy penalty clauses for late delivery and try to pay in stages linked to construction milestones.
wild_tether
Replied: 02.02.2026 11:42
The notary is a neutral party in Bulgaria; they don't represent your interests, they only verify the legality of the transaction. You absolutely need your own legal representative to review the Title Deed and ensure the seller actually has the right to sell the property.
