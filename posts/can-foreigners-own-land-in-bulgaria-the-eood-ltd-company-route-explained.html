Can Foreigners Own Land in Bulgaria? (The EOOD / LTD Company Route Explained)

shadow_weaver
Topic Starter
Posted: 20.01.2025 00:42
I am a non-EU citizen looking to invest in a vacation property in Bulgaria, but I have heard there are restrictions on purchasing land directly as an individual. I understand that setting up an EOOD (a local limited company) is the standard workaround, but I am confused about the legal requirements and ongoing maintenance costs. Could someone explain the process of registering a company for this purpose and whether it is truly worth the bureaucratic hassle? Any advice from expats who have already navigated this route would be greatly appreciated.

I went the EOOD route back in 2018. The setup cost is relatively low, but you really need a good accountant. It is worth it if you plan to keep the property long-term, but just remember that you also need to understand annual property taxes in Bulgaria before you commit to the purchase.
velvet_coder
Replied: 25.02.2025 08:42
Setting up an EOOD is the standard way for non-EU citizens to own land. It’s not as scary as it sounds, but the maintenance involves annual filing fees and accounting costs. Don't skip the due diligence; always check if the building has its certificate of occupancy. You should read this guide on what Act 16 in Bulgaria is before signing anything.
urban_echo
Replied: 09.03.2025 13:19
The bureaucracy is definitely a headache, especially for a foreigner. If you are just buying a flat, you don't need a company, but for a house with land, an EOOD is mandatory. My advice? Hire a local lawyer to handle the registration; it saves you months of frustration.
frost_byte
Replied: 30.04.2025 10:05
I did this for a rural plot. The company setup was fine, but the ongoing tax reporting is the real chore. Make sure your accountant knows you aren't actually trading; they need to file 'dormant' status reports if the company isn't generating income.
arctic_nomad
Replied: 16.05.2025 12:32
Is it worth the hassle? If you love the country and want a specific piece of land, yes. Just be prepared for the fact that you are now a business owner in the eyes of the Bulgarian state. You'll have to pay social security if you become the manager, so clarify that with your accountant first.
phantom_gear
Replied: 15.06.2025 14:33
If you are looking at a fixer-upper, the EOOD route is just the beginning. The real cost comes when you start the repairs. I highly recommend checking out some articles on buying a rural village house because the renovation reality often differs from the dream.
zenith_flow
Replied: 13.08.2025 07:46
I've owned an EOOD for six years now. The annual maintenance costs me about 500-800 BGN depending on the accountant's fees. It’s manageable, but don't try to register the company yourself. Use an agency that specializes in expat services.
glass_horizon
Replied: 25.09.2025 09:14
The process takes about two weeks if your documents are in order. The main 'hassle' is just the annual bookkeeping. If you don't have a local partner you trust, stay away from complex structures and stick to a simple EOOD.
ember_quest
Replied: 06.12.2025 14:56
I found the whole process surprisingly straightforward once I hired a professional. You just need a local address for the company and a bank account. The bank account part is actually the hardest part for non-EU citizens due to anti-money laundering regulations!
