Understanding the Bulgarian Notarial Deed (Notarialen Akt): Crucial Clauses to Check

ember_quest
Topic Starter
Posted: 20.01.2025 10:37
I am currently in the process of purchasing a property in Bulgaria and have just received the draft Notarialen Akt from the seller's lawyer. I want to ensure that I am fully protected, but the legal jargon is quite difficult to navigate as an expat. Could anyone advise on which specific clauses are non-negotiable to verify, especially regarding encumbrances or existing utility debts? I would appreciate any tips on what to look for before signing at the notary office.

Replied: 11.02.2025 07:41
Always ensure the deed includes a 'free of encumbrances' clause. The notary is legally required to check the Property Register, but double-check that no mortgages or distraints are listed. If you are looking for long-term advice, this guide on hidden costs and legal fees is essential reading.
atlas_notes
Replied: 12.02.2025 00:58
Check the 'Tezhestiti' (Encumbrances) section carefully. It should state that the property is free from any third-party rights. If you're looking at coastal areas, check out this post on buying property in Nessebar to understand the local market nuances.
phantom_gear
Replied: 12.02.2025 01:23
Make sure the seller provides a document from the local municipality proving that there are no outstanding property taxes. You should also verify the utility bills (electricity and water) by asking for the latest payment receipts directly from the providers.
arctic_nomad
Replied: 12.03.2025 13:31
The Notarialen Akt must contain the exact address, cadastral number, and the size of the property as stated in the Cadastral Map. Discrepancies here can cause massive headaches later when you try to register the property in your name.
glass_horizon
Replied: 26.03.2025 09:47
Never rely solely on the seller's lawyer. Hire your own independent lawyer who specializes in Bulgarian property law to review the draft. It is a small price to pay compared to the risk of inheriting a hidden debt.
opal_circuit
Replied: 09.05.2025 16:04
Check for 'vazhdeli' or existing rental agreements that might be registered against the property. Sometimes a property is sold with a long-term tenant, and the new owner must honor that contract.
mercury_glow
Replied: 15.05.2025 00:48
Ask the notary to explain the 'Eviction' clause. It should explicitly state that the seller must vacate the property by a specific date, otherwise, you might have trouble getting them to leave after the money is transferred.
kinetic_soul
Replied: 13.06.2025 13:15
Verify the 'Price' clause. Ensure it matches exactly what you agreed upon. Some sellers try to declare a lower price on the deed to save on taxes, but this is illegal and puts you at a huge disadvantage if you ever decide to sell.
atlas_notes
Replied: 25.07.2025 02:56
The deed should clearly state the seller's marital status. If the property was acquired during marriage, both spouses must sign the deed to make the sale valid.
echo_point
Replied: 29.07.2025 11:47
Before you sign anything, demand a 'Certificate of Absence of Encumbrances' issued by the Registry Agency no more than 24-48 hours before the signing date. This is the only way to be 100% sure.
peak_seeker
Replied: 21.08.2025 14:28
Check if the property has a 'Right of Use' (pravo na polzvane) granted to anyone else. This is a common trap in Bulgarian real estate that can prevent you from using the property even if you own the title.
dusk_pilot
Replied: 12.11.2025 09:30
Don't forget to check the 'Act 16' (Usage Permit) if you are buying a new build. Without it, you might struggle to get permanent utility contracts or insurance.
atlas_notes
Replied: 28.11.2025 06:48
If you are buying an older property, ensure there are no illegal structural modifications. You might want to read about renovating an old Bulgarian house to see how common these issues are.
drift_wood
Replied: 31.12.2025 03:50
Ensure the 'Technical Passport' of the building is available. It contains vital info about the state of the building's infrastructure, which is a legal requirement for newer properties.
frost_byte
Replied: 01.02.2026 02:32
Always have a Bulgarian translator present at the notary office, even if you speak some Bulgarian. Legal terminology is very specific, and you need to understand every word being read out loud.
