Opening a Bank Account in Bulgaria as a Foreign Non-Resident: Which Banks Say Yes?

urban_echo
Topic Starter
Posted: 30.01.2025 06:31
I am planning to relocate to Bulgaria soon and I am currently looking for information on which major banks are the most foreigner-friendly for non-residents. I have heard that many institutions have tightened their compliance policies, making it quite difficult to open a personal account without a permanent residence permit. Does anyone have recent experience with specific branches or banks in Sofia that are more accommodating to expats? Any advice on the necessary documentation or potential hurdles would be greatly appreciated.

It has definitely become harder. I had luck with UniCredit Bulbank in Sofia, but they required a signed rental agreement and a letter of employment. Honestly, if you are looking to settle down long-term, it's worth reading up on considering Bulgaria for retirement to understand the residency requirements better before you even step into a branch.
arctic_nomad
Replied: 18.04.2025 02:01
Avoid the smaller branches; go to the main headquarters in the city center. I opened an account at DSK Bank as a non-resident, but it took three visits and a lot of patience. Be prepared to prove the source of your funds.
fable_runner
Replied: 04.05.2025 03:01
ProCredit Bank is generally the most expat-friendly because they deal with international clients frequently. They are strict on compliance but transparent about what they need.

I was told 'no' by three different banks before finding a helpful clerk at Postbank. It really depends on who is behind the desk that day. Bring your passport, a utility bill from your home country, and your employment contract.

If you are moving here to buy property, the process is slightly different. Check out this guide on notary fees and closing costs in Bulgaria, as sometimes having a local property deed helps prove your ties to the country when applying for an account.

Honestly, I gave up on traditional banks and started using Revolut and Wise for my day-to-day. Once you get your residence permit, opening a local account becomes 10 times easier.

Raiffeisen (now part of DSK) was decent, but they really wanted to see a local phone number and a proof of address. Don't bother going without a Bulgarian SIM card.
cipher_mist

I had a nightmare experience trying to open an account without a long-term residency card. Most banks will treat you as a 'high-risk' client due to AML regulations. Expect them to ask for tax IDs from your home country.
nova_sketch
Replied: 08.11.2025 04:54
Stick to the major players like UniCredit or DSK. They have the most robust procedures for foreigners. Just make sure you bring a translator if your documents aren't in Bulgarian or English, as some branches are very strict about notarized translations.
